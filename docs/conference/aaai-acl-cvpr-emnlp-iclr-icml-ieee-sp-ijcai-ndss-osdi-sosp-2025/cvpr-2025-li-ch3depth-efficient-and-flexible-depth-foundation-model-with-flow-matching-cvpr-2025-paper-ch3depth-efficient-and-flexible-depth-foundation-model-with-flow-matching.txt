# Abstract 

Depth estimation is a fundamental task in 3D vision. An ideal depth estimation model is expected to embrace metic-ulous detail, temporal consistency, and high efficiency. Al-though existing foundation models can perform well in cer-tain specific aspects, most of them fall short of fulfilling all the above requirements simultaneously. In this paper, we present CH 3Depth, an efficient and flexible model for depth estimation with flow matching to address this chal-lenge. Specifically, 1) we reframe the optimization objec-tive of flow matching as the Inversion by Direct Iteration

(InDI) to improve accuracy. 2) To enhance efficiency, we propose non-uniform sampling to achieve better prediction with fewer sampling steps. 3) We design the Latent Tem-poral Stabilizer (LTS) to enhance temporal consistency by aggregating latent codes of adjacent frames, enabling our method to be lightweight and compatible for video depth es-timation. CH 3Depth achieves state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot evaluations across multiple image and video datasets, excelling in prediction accuracy, efficiency, and temporal consistency, highlighting its potential as the next foundation model for depth estimation. Depth estimation is a vital task in 3D vision, serving as the prerequisite of robot navigation [26, 51], autonomous driving [5, 7], mixed reality [27, 32], and content gener-ation [62, 65]. Considering the demands of these appli-cations, an ideal depth model is supposed to predict accu-rate depth with several characteristics. Firstly, the predicted depth should exhibit meticulous details [28, 29, 64], facili-tating the synthesis of vivid contents. Secondly, maintain-ing temporal consistency [20, 39, 49] across video frames is essential for video-based applications [32, 50, 63]. Besides, improving model efficiency can benefit platforms with lim-ited resources, e.g. , robotics [17, 41] and vehicles [7, 19]. In pursuit of these characteristics, several recent depth estimation models [1, 2, 18, 24, 49, 55, 56, 59] are de-signed and proposed. However, they struggle to simulta-neously satisfy the aforementioned demands. For instance, Lotus [18] and Depth Anything [55] can predict fine details but produce flickering results on videos. Marigold [24] is time-consuming due to their diffusion-based sampling strat-egy. NVDS [49, 50] effectively enforces video consistency at the cost of losing details. Overall, how to efficiently de-rive both meticulous and consistent depth through a unified framework remains a challenging question. In response to the challenge, we propose CH 3Depth, a depth foundation model for efficient and flexible depth estimation. To predict meticulous and accurate depth, we pro-pose using Inversion by Direct Iteration (InDI) [9, 25] in CH 3Depth. Previous DepthFM [16] makes an initial at-tempt to introduce flow matching [30] into depth estima-tion. They adopt the default globally fixed-scale velocity field [30] as the optimization objective. However, in each sampling, DepthFM needs to predict the global path from the source noise to the target distribution, resulting in a waste of network capacity. Considering the problem, we use InDI to better focus on the optimal transport towards the target distribution based on current distribution. It is equiv-alent to assigning ELBO coefficients to inputs of different noise levels during the training phase. InDI flow matching favors the situation of difficult initial denoising, achieving higher accuracy and finer details. To enhance model efficiency, we design a non-uniform sampling strategy for flow matching. Previous diffusion-based [18, 24] and flow-matching-based [16] methods typ-ically use uniform steps for inference, which contradicts the common practices in model optimization [43, 44]. The denoising process should pursue rapid convergence in the initial stages, followed by fine-tuning with small steps in later phases. Therefore, we employ a definable concave function which maps uniform sampling to nonlinear steps. In this way, our CH 3Depth can predict more accurate and meticulous depth with fewer sampling steps than prior arts [16, 20, 24], improving both efficiency and accuracy. The final step involves enforcing temporal consistency on video sequences. Since CH 3Depth operates within the latent space, whereas prior video depth methods are based on RGB features [61] or depth maps [49], they cannot be directly integrated into our framework. For this issue, we propose the Latent Temporal Stabilizer (LTS), which aggre-gates the latent codes of predicted depth in adjacent frames. Besides, to improve the model generalization ability, the consistent deviation loss is designed to leverage natural-scene videos [45, 49] to train the LTS module, which can align the temporal dimension and improve the stability. As a consequence, CH 3Depth is adaptable for single-image and video depth estimation. We carry out exten-sive experiments on multiple datasets. As shown in Fig. 1, CH 3Depth outperforms prior arts [16, 18, 24] in depth accu-racy, edge details, video consistency, and model efficiency. Besides, LTS can work with other diffusion-based or flow-matching-based models [16, 18] to enhance consistency, which further showcase the flexibility of our approach. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows: • We propose CH 3Depth, an efficient and flexible depth foundation model with flow matching. • InDI flow matching with non-uniform sampling is de-signed to improve accuracy and efficiency. • We introduce the latent temporal stabilizer and consistent deviation loss to enforce consistency in the latent space. 

# 2. Related Work 

Image Depth Estimation. Discriminative models have held a dominant position in image depth estimation for an extended period. MiDaS [34] and DPT [35] achieve gen-eralized depth estimation through a multi-dataset training strategy. Depth Anything [55, 56] greatly increases the scale of training data and achieves a foundation model. However, this pixel-wise regression-based paradigm can-not directly handle high-resolution inputs due to computa-tional cost, resulting in the loss of high-frequency details. Marigold [24] successfully introduces diffusion models into depth estimation and achieves impressive results. Its suc-cessor [18] enhances the efficiency defect to some extent, but it still fails to balance speed and detail precision well. DepthFM [16] initially tries to transform the diffusion-based model into flow matching and achieves preliminary results. This leads us to recognize that flow-matching-based models hold the promise of emerging as the next foundation models for depth estimation because of its flexible training paradigms and fast inference. Thus CH 3Depth further im-prove efficiency and spatial details through InDI optimiza-tion objective and non-uniform sampling.

Flow Matching. Flow matching, with its concise imple-mentation and efficient inference, becomes a potential alter-native to diffusion models and gradually gains an advantage in fields such as image generation [12, 13, 30] and video un-derstanding [6]. Diffusion-based depth estimation networks need Gaussian noise as the initial distribution, which may not intuitively conform to the natural correlation between the image and the target depth map distribution. We use flow matching to construct the linear optimal transport be-tween the noisy image and the supervised target, providing faster sampling while enhancing spatial details. 

# 3. CH 3Depth Framework 

In Sec. 3.1, the overall structure of CH 3Depth is briefly pre-sented for overall comprehension. In Sec. 3.2, we explain the InDI flow matching and non-uniform sampling to im-prove accuracy and efficiency. The Latent Temporal Stabi-lizer (LTS) for temporal consistency is described in Sec. 3.3. We also explain implementation details in Sec. 3.4. 

3.1. Overview 

CH 3Depth is dedicated to building a flow-matching-based [30, 31] depth estimation foundation model simulta-neously realizing high-resolution fine-grained, temporally consistent, and efficient depth estimation in a flexible ar-chitecture. Fig. 2 showcases the technical pipeline of CH 3Depth framework. First, a RGB image Ik from a se-quence of length K, where k ∈ { 0, 1, · · · , K − 1} is com-pressed by a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) [37] to reduce the resolution, obtaining the RGB latent code zkc . A InDI flow matching network Nf m is trained to estimate the opti-mal transport from the input distribution to the ground truth depth latent code zd, thereby generating the initial depth la-tent code ˆzd for image depth estimation or zkd init for subse-quent video depth estimation. The LTS is a lightweight flow matching model that optimizes the temporal consistency of the initial zkd init in a sliding window manner to get ˆzkd with window size w. All combined result yields temporally con-sistent fine-grained depth estimation.

Preliminaries. Flow matching models [30, 31] are gen-erative models that learn to regress vector fields along fixed conditional probability paths, enabling transformation across data distributions. We leverage its inherent straight sampling trajectory nature to construct the corruption pro-cess with standard Gaussian noise ϵ:

ϕt(zd) = tz d + (1 − t)ϵ , (2) where ϕt(zd) stands for the corrupted data, with t ∈ [0 , 1] ,serving as the timestep for the straight-line path. According to the prior knowledge of uniform linear transformation, the time-dependent velocity field at time t can be obtained as: 

vt(ϕt(zd)) = ϕt(zd) − ϵ . (3) Based on the relationship between distance and velocity, the ordinary differential equation (ODE) is given by: 

dϕ t(zd) = vt(ϕt(zd)) dt . (4) Previous works [16, 54] make Nf m directly learn the glob-ally fixed velocity field from Gaussian noise to the target depth distribution to simply achieve linear optimal transport during inference. The loss function can be expressed as: 

LF M = Et∥N f m (ϕt(zd), z c, t ) − vt(ϕ1(zd)) ∥2 . (5) During the inference phase, by predicting vt(ϕ1(zd)) =

zd − ϵ through the network Nf m and performing numer-ical integration from t ∈ [0 , 1] , we can recover the depth latent codes from Gaussian noise with several steps. 

Inversion by Direct Iteration(InDI) Flow Matching. For depth estimation, training the network to predict a fixed-scale mapping from Gaussian noise to the target distribution is unreasonable. On the one hand, during inference, even if partial denoising has been achieved, the network Nf m still needs to predict the global path, resulting in a waste of ca-pacity. On the other hand, during the training phase, the ELBO coefficients of Optimal Transport [30] are also ex-plored to be changed to something else better [25]. There-fore, while maintaining the previously mentioned prior of linear optimal transport, we rewrite Eq. (5) to achieve InDI flow matching: 

LInDI = Et∥N f m (ϕt(zd), z c, t ) − (ϕ1(zd) − ϕt(zd)) ∥2 .

(6) The optimization objective ϕ1(zd) − ϕt(zd) allows the flow matching network to directly predict the linear optimal transport from the current distribution to the target distribu-tion at variable scales, rather than a fixed-scale global map-ping starting from the Gaussian distribution. Under ideal conditions where the prediction is unbiased, the result of our 

Nf m supervised by InDI flow matching (denoted as N ours f m )can be linearly transformed into the result from previous work(denoted as N f ixed f m ): 

N ours f m (ϕt(zd), z c, t ) = N f ixed f m (ϕt(zd), z c, t )·(1 −t) . (7) This proves again that the InDI flow matching also con-forms to linear optimal transport. Meanwhile, Eq. (7) indi-cates that the InDI flow matching is equivalent to the prod-uct of the previous loss function and 1 − t. This is reflected in the derivation in [25] as an adjustment of the ELBO coef-ficients. The loss coefficients assigned to the different noisy level of inputs during training are crucial for flow match-ing. Our experiments further demonstrate the superiority of InDI [9] over FM-OT [30] in the field of depth estimation.

3.3. Latent Temporal Stabilizer (LTS) 

We adopt a lightweight UNet serving as LTS to aggre-gate initial depth latent codes predicted by the preceding 

Nf m with a sliding window size of w to obtain consis-tent depth predictions. Besides training LTS using the flow matching training paradigm metioned above on synthetic datasets [4, 23, 33, 36, 47], in order to improve its gener-alization, we propose temporal consistent deviation loss to assist the training on natural video depth datasets [45, 49]. 7225 Temporal Stabilizer. LTS takes the depth latent codes 

{zk−id init |i = 0 , 1, · · · , w } within window size w, and the target frame image latent variable zkc as inputs, to restore temporally consistent predicted latent code ˆzkd . Unlike the aforementioned flow matching network Nf m , the training source distribution designed by network LTS is not a mix-ture of Gaussian noise and ground truth latent variables, but a slightly noisy zkd init . Because in this case, the source distribution is already relatively close to the target distribu-tion, and only the consistency needs to be optimized. At the same time, adding noise to the input distribution of the flow matching model can greatly improve the generalization of the model, according to the previous works [13, 16, 30, 31]. 

Temporal Consistent Deviation Loss. Due to the lack of high-frame-rate and diverse synthetic video depth datasets, LTS needs to be fine-tuned by mixing natural scene data [45, 49], besides training on limited synthetic datasets [4, 23, 33, 36, 47]. When training on synthetic data, flow matching loss is enough to match consistency because the annotation is definitely accurate. However, this is not feasible for natural scene data because the annotations from stereo matching [22, 53] or sensors [40, 66] are not ideally precise. 3.4. Implementation details 

Network Architecture. The VAE defaults to an 8× down-sampling version, and its output is a 4-channel latent code. All network designs are based on the diffuser library. Nf m 

uses an 8-channel to 4-channel conditional U-net to re-ceive the concatenated input of image latent codes and noisy depth latent codes. Its structure is consistent with prior arts [16, 24]. LTS uses a lightweight unconditional U-net with input channel as 4( w + 2) and output channel as 4,where w is the sliding window size. Compared to Nf m ,the additional 4w channels of the input are the depth latent codes within the window. The initial distribution is the 50 -step noisy initial predicted depth latent code zkd init with the same noise adding method as Stable Diffusion [37]. 

Training Procedure. We use a mixture of multiple syn-thetic datasets [4, 21, 36, 42, 47] to train Nf m . After Nf m 

converges, we further mix synthetic and natural scene video depth data [23, 33, 45, 49] to train LTS. The training re-ceipts are consistent with Marigold [24]. It takes a total of 

4K effective iterations with a batch size of 128 . We adopt the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 3 · 10 −5,which is reduced to 3 · 10 −7 after 3K iterations.

Training Datasets. The training data we use for flow matching model Nf m is consistent with previous works [18, 24], including Hypersim [36] and Virtual Kitti 2 [4]. Hy-persim is an indoor synthetic dataset containing 461 scenes. We use 54K samples for training as in Marigold [24]. The 20K training samples of the autonomous driving synthetic dataset Virtual Kitti 2 contain five outdoor scenes with dif-ferent weathers and camera settings. Owing to the restricted diversity of outdoor scenes in Virtual Kitti 2, additional 22K synthetic samples is employed, randomly sampled from Tartanair [47], SHIFT [42], and MVS-Synth [21]. The model performances of the two versions of training data are reported independently. Finally, the synthetic video depth datasets [23, 33, 46, 47] as well as the natural scene video depth datasets VDW [49] and WSVD [45] are utilized to train LTS with the temporal consistent deviation loss.

# 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose CH 3Depth, a flexible flow-matching-based depth estimation foundation model achiev-ing efficient, meticulous detail, and temporally consistent predictions. Our method shifts the optimization objective of flow matching from FM-OT to InDI, enabling model to directly predict the transformation from current distribution to target distribution. Non-uniform sampling is proposed to improve efficiency by reducing sampling steps. CH 3Depth is made compatible with both image and video depth es-timation through Latent Temporal Stabilizer (LTS), aggre-gating the predicted depth latent codes of adjacent frames in the latent space to enhance temporal consistency. Tem-poral consistent deviation loss enables LTS to be trained on natural scene datasets, further enhancing generalization.
