## Abstract 

Commercial greenhouse cucumber production is graded primarily by fruit length, where accu-rate per-fruit measurement drives harvest scheduling, labour allocation, and logistics planning. Conventional manual measurement using thread or caliper is accurate but throughput-limited and infeasible at commercial scale. This paper presents CucumberVision, a non-contact length estimation framework that uses an Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D camera to detect, segment, and measure greenhouse cucumbers in real time. A YOLO26n instance segmentation model locates cu-cumbers, and the Segment Anything Model (SAM) with a ViT-B backbone refines each detection to a pixel-precise binary mask. Five length estimation methods are implemented and evaluated under matched conditions: (M1) a dominant-axis skeleton scan-line baseline; (M2) Principal Component Analysis on the bounding-box depth point cloud; (M3) SAM mask with medial-axis skeletonisation; (M4) a hybrid keypoint-guided approach using a YOLO26-pose model predicting five anatomical landmarks (KP0–KP4) with piecewise 3D arc-length approximation; and (M5) a novel medial arc spline method that fits a cubic spline through the 3D medial axis of the SAM mask and computes arc length by trapezoidal integration the first such application to elongated 1

All five methods share a common capture pipeline comprising five-frame burst depth averaging, colour-stream intrinsic alignment, and adaptive method selection with cascading fallbacks ensuring 100% measurement coverage. A benchmark dataset of 48 captures across seven cucumbers in three size categories (small ∼8 cm, medium ∼13 cm, large ∼25 cm) with paired thread-based ground truth is used to compare all methods under identical conditions. # 1 Introduction 

Global cucumber ( Cucumis sativus L.) production exceeded 88 million tonnes in 2023, making it the world’s fourth most-produced vegetable [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, 2023]. In tem-perate regions, commercial production is predominantly conducted under protected cultivation. Cucumber quality is graded primarily by fruit length: European greenhouse standards commonly recognise Class I (25–35 cm), Class II (20–25 cm) and out-of-grade, with per-kilogram prices differing by 20–40 % between classes. Because cucumbers elongate at approximately 2–4 cm/d under optimal conditions [Walsh et al., 2021], the number of fruit reaching a target length class within one to three days can be forecast from their current length distribution, enabling growers to schedule labour, pre-allocate packaging, and commit to distribution partners in advance. The conventional measurement workflow a worker with a digital caliper measuring each fruit individually achieves high accuracy but is throughput-limited at 300–400 fruit per hour. Horticultural labour costs rose 30–50 % between 2013 and 2023 [Walsh et al., 2021], intensifying pressure to automate inspection tasks. Consumer-grade depth cameras now achieve sub-centimetre accuracy at 0.3–1.5 m, integrate compactly with mobile robots, and retail for under USD 300 [Rijal et al., 2024]. However, camera-based length estimation accuracy is limited not by depth precision alone but by the algorithm used to identify the fruit’s length axis. Five principal algorithmic families have emerged, four established and one proposed here: 1. Dominant-axis skeleton sampling. Pixels sampled along the longer bounding-box axis are deprojected to 3-D [Wang and Li, 2014, Walsh et al., 2021]. Simple and fast but systematically underestimates diagonally oriented fruit due to cosine projection error. 2. Point-cloud PCA. All depth-valid pixels are deprojected and the principal axis identified via SVD [Chen et al., 2022, Patel et al., 2025]. Orientation-invariant but susceptible to background leakage. 3. Foundation-model segmentation with medial-axis skeletonisation. SAM [Kirillov et al., 2023] yields a pixel-precise mask whose medial-axis skeleton estimates the longitudinal axis [Zhang et al., 2023a]. Eliminates background leakage but still measures a straight-line chord. 4. Semantic keypoint regression. A pose network predicts anatomical landmarks deprojected to 3-D and connected by piecewise segments [Chen et al., 2022, Ren et al., 2024]. Accurate under occlusion but requires keypoint annotations and underestimates arc length. 5. 3D medial arc spline fitting (proposed). The SAM mask’s medial axis is deprojected, cross-section centroids computed, and a chord-length-parameterised cubic spline fitted. Arc length is obtained by numerical integration, providing continuous curvature-aware measurement the first such application to elongated vegetable sizing. 3No published study has evaluated all five families under matched conditions. This paper addresses this gap through five contributions: 1. Unified five-method pipeline. CucumberVision implements M1–M5 sharing a single D435 driver, YOLO26n detector, SAM ViT-B segmentor, burst depth averaging, and colour-stream intrinsic alignment. 2. Novel curvature-aware method. M5 deprojects SAM-masked medial-axis pixels into 3D, fits a cubic spline through cross-section centroids, and integrates arc length—the first such application to vegetable length measurement. 3. Controlled comparative evaluation. All five methods benchmarked across three size categories with Wilcoxon tests (Bonferroni-corrected α = 0 .0125 ) and bootstrap 95% CIs. 4. Adaptive method selector. A cascading decision tree assigns the most accurate method per cucumber with 100% coverage. 5. Deployable dashboard. CucumberVision Streamlit app with live camera and upload modes, per-fruit visualisation, and CSV/Excel export. The remainder is organised as follows. Section 2 reviews related work. Section 3 presents the methodology. Section 4 describes the experimental protocol. Section 5 presents results and deployment. Section 6 discusses findings and limitations. Section 7 concludes. 

# 2 Related Work 

## 2.1 Non-contact fruit and vegetable sizing 

Wang and Li [2014] estimated sweet onion diameter from a Kinect v1 using bounding-box projection, forming the conceptual basis of M1. Walsh et al. [2021] compared four depth sensors for orchard sizing, concluding that the D435i offered the best accuracy below 1.5 m. Liu et al. [2019] applied Mask R-CNN to greenhouse cucumbers with stereo depth, reporting 10–15 % length errors. Lawal [2024] compared YOLO variants for cucurbit detection, motivating the use of YOLO26. Koirala et al. [2022] compared six methods for mango sizing, finding 3-D approaches substantially outperformed 2-D methods. Song et al. [2023] measured cucumber phenotypes using RGB-D, reporting 4–6 % MAPE. Hong et al. [2024] achieved sub-centimetre accuracy for oriental melon via multi-view fusion but required impractical infrastructure. Türkseven et al. [2021] demonstrated D435 suitability for greenhouse tasks. Singh et al. [2024, 2025] deployed the D435 for tomato flower detection on robotic platforms. 

## 2.2 Deep-learning detectors and segmenters 

The YOLO family [Redmon et al., 2016] established single-stage detection at real-time speed. Wang et al. [2024] extended YOLO with a 6-DoF pose head for harvesting. The Ultralytics framework [Ultralytics, 2024] provides the training backbone used here. Liu et al. [2019] reported 85.3% mAP for cucumber detection with Mask R-CNN [He et al., 2017], but its higher latency motivated the present use of YOLO26n with integrated segmentation. 

## 2.3 SAM in agricultural applications 

SAM [Kirillov et al., 2023] segments arbitrary objects given a prompt. Carraro et al. [2023] identified SAM as transformative for smart farming. Williams et al. [2024] demonstrated competitive zero-shot leaf segmentation. Kaur et al. [2025] deployed a visual-language transformer for greenhouse disease detection, establishing the Streamlit dashboard paradigm adopted here. SAM 2 [Ravi et al., 2024] offers a path to multi-frame smoothing discussed in Section 6.6. In this work, SAM refines YOLO26 detections to pixel-precise masks for M3–M5. 

## 2.4 Keypoint detection for produce measurement 

Chen et al. [2022] applied keypoint detection to vegetable sizing, reporting 1.5–3.2 % MAPE. Ren et al. [2024] extended the approach to irregular root vegetables. The present work implements a YOLO26-pose head with five keypoints per cucumber integrated into an adaptive selector. 4

## 2.5 Medial-axis and spline-based measurement 

The medial-axis algorithm of Lee et al. [1994] extracts topological centre-lines and has been applied to fish length [Zhang et al., 2023a], rice panicles [Wu et al., 2019], wheat [Aich and Stavness, 2017], and maize ears [Bao et al., 2019]. However, 2D arc length suffers perspective distortion [Paulus, 2019]. Spline fitting provides rigorous 3D arc-length computation [de Boor, 2001, Farin, 2002]: Paulus [2019] fitted B-splines to barley ears, Jin et al. [2018] to maize stalks, and Magistri et al. [2022] to crop rows. Tagliasacchi et al. [2016] reviewed 3D skeleton extraction from point clouds. No prior study has combined SAM segmentation with 3D medial-axis extraction, centroid binning, and cubic spline arc-length integration for vegetable measurement. M5 fills this gap. 

## 2.6 Gap analysis 

Three gaps motivate this work. Gap 1: No controlled multi-method comparison of all five algorithmic families on the same cucumber dataset. Gap 2: No published method computes 3D arc length of curved vegetables via spline fitting despite 1–4% chord-to-arc bias for typical cucumber curvature radii (30–60 cm).

# 3 Methodology 

This section describes the CucumberVision pipeline from sensor acquisition through detection, segmentation, and the five measurement methods. Fig. 1 provides an overview. 

## 3.1 Hardware Configuration 

The Intel RealSense D435 active-stereo RGB-D camera was deployed in two environments: a controlled laboratory and a commercial greenhouse. The camera was positioned 0.4–0.9 m from the canopy. Table 2 summarises key parameters. At each position, a 5-frame burst was acquired. Depth frames were pixel-wise median-averaged ( ∼44% noise reduction); the sharpest colour frame was selected by Laplacian variance [Pech-Pacheco et al., 2000].

3.3.6 Adaptive method selector 

Different cucumbers require different methods. Fig. 9 shows the cascading selector. 

Decision 1 (primary): If a SAM mask is available and contains ≥100 valid depth points, M5 (medial arc spline) is applied as the primary method. 

Decision 2: If keypoints are additionally reliable ( vi ≥ 0.50 for KP0 and KP4), M4 (hybrid keypoint) is also computed and logged for comparison, but M5 remains primary. 

Decision 3: If a SAM mask is available but point density falls below the M5 threshold ( <100 points), M3 (SAM + PCA) is applied. 

Decision 4: If no SAM mask is available, the bounding-box aspect ratio is evaluated against a threshold of 1.8, routing to M2 (PCA depth cloud, orientation-invariant) for diagonally oriented cucumbers ( h/w < 1.8)or M1 (skeleton scan-line, axis-aligned) for vertically or horizontally oriented specimens ( h/w ≥ 1.8). The full priority chain is M5 → M4 → M3 → M2 → M1, with a cross-fallback (M1 ↔ M2) guaranteeing 100% measurement coverage. The method ID, activation reason, and fallback path are logged per cucumber in the output CSV. On the 48-capture benchmark, M5 was activated on 100% of captures (all had SAM masks with ≥100 valid depth points); M4 additionally processed 79% of captures (38/48) where keypoint confidence was sufficient. 11

## 4.2 Ground-truth collection protocol 

Ground-truth lengths were measured using a flexible, non-stretchable thread laid along the dorsal (outer) midline of each cucumber from stem end to blossom end, then transferred to a calibrated steel rule (Fig. 11). This thread-tracing method captures the true arc length of the curved fruit surface, unlike a rigid vernier caliper which measures only the straight-line chord and therefore systematically underestimates curved speci-mens by the same chord-to-arc bias that motivates M5. Each cucumber was measured three times; the mean was recorded as Lgt [Song et al., 2023, Chen et al., 2022]. The thread method has an operator variability of 

±1–2 mm depending on how tightly the thread conforms to surface curvature. 13

## 4.4 Streamlit Dashboard and Operator Workflow 

A Streamlit dashboard integrates the capture pipeline, adaptive selector, and measurement output. The operator specifies row and position; the system executes burst capture, processes frames through YOLO26n + SAM, applies adaptive method selection, and performs 3D deduplication across stops. Annotated images, per-fruit lengths, and aggregate statistics are displayed; all data are exported as CSV and Excel.

# 6 Discussion 

## 6.1 Summary of Key Findings 

M5 achieves the lowest error on all metrics (MAE 0.58 cm, MAPE 4.13%, R2 = 0 .98 ), significantly outper-forming all methods ( α = 0 .0125 ). Accuracy improves geometric sophistication (by descending error): M1 (9.68%) → M2 (5.31%) → M3 (5.82%) → M4 (5.51%) → M5 (4.13%). The adaptive selector ensures 100% coverage. The intrinsic alignment correction eliminates a systematic 12–18% underestimation. 

## 6.2 Method-Specific Analysis 

M1 serves as a fast baseline (28 ms, 100% success) but produces the highest MAPE (9.68%) due to cosine projection error, with deviations >2 cm on large specimens. M2 eliminates orientation dependence (MAPE 5.31%) but admits background pixels. M3’s SAM mask removes ∼70% of background; however, on small cucumbers ( ≤8 cm) the mask occasionally bleeds onto adjacent green leaves, introducing noisy pixels that pull the PCA axis off-centre and increase MAE above M2 (0.96 cm vs. 0.83 cm). The SAM mask benefit is most pronounced for medium and large cucumbers where mask precision is high; for small cucumbers, M2’s raw bounding-box cloud can be cleaner than a noisy SAM mask. M4 recovers through semantic keypoints (MAE 0.85 cm) with piecewise curvature correction, though 21% fallback rate reveals keypoint fragility. M5 achieves best results (MAE 0.58 cm, no outliers >2 cm) via continuous spline integration with unique occlusion robustness through gap interpolation. 17 

Figure 15: Five-method comparison on new 5-cucumber validation set (GT 14.72–38.0 cm). Each row shows the same cucumber processed by M1 (skeleton), M2 (PCA), M3 (SAM+PCA), M4 (keypoints), and M5 (medial arc). M5 matches thread-based GT exactly for all 5 samples. 

## 6.3 Practical Implications 

The adaptive selector guarantees measurements regardless of scene complexity. M1/M2 execute within 30 ms (viable on Jetson); M3–M5 require SAM ( ∼200 ms/frame, amortised), feasible on RTX 3060 but needing TensorRT for edge deployment. The selector degrades gracefully on constrained hardware. 

## 6.4 Comparison with Prior Work 

M5’s MAPE of 4.13% compares favourably with Song et al. [2023] (4–6%) and Chen et al. [2022] (1.5– 3.2%, different sensor/protocol). The controlled five-method comparison under identical conditions and the adaptive selector are novel contributions. 

## 6.5 Limitations 

The YOLO training dataset comprised 1,500 annotated images (4,360 instances, Table 3), used solely to train the YOLO26n detector. The method evaluation benchmark is a separate dataset of 48 captures across seven individual cucumbers spanning three discrete size clusters (GT 6.9–25.0 cm), collected using the thread measurement protocol. An additional 5-cucumber validation set (GT 14.72–38.0 cm, Fig. 15) was collected after the primary benchmark to demonstrate method behaviour across a wider length range, including specimens ≥25 cm not present in the primary benchmark. Several limitations apply to the current study. First, the 48 capture benchmark is sufficient for method ranking but does not support claims of absolute generalisation performance. The 15–20 cm length range is uncharacterised in the primary benchmark, though partially covered by the validation set (14.72 cm and 15.52 cm specimens). Second, the reported R2 = 0 .98 is partially inflated by the between-cluster contrast inherent in a three-group ground-truth distribution; Pearson r = 0 .998 is the more informative measure of predictive accuracy. Third, all data were collected with a single sensor (Intel RealSense D435) across two greenhouse environments, which limits cross-platform and cross-variety conclusions. Fourth, thread-based ground truth carries an operator variability of ±1–2 mm depending on how tightly the thread conforms to surface curvature, introducing a small irreducible uncertainty in the ground-truth values. ## 6.6 Future Work 

An expanded dataset with continuous coverage and held-out test set is needed. FastSAM [Zhao et al., 2023] or MobileSAM [Zhang et al., 2023b] would enable edge deployment. SAM 2 [Ravi et al., 2024] offers multi-frame 19 smoothing. Length distributions can forecast harvest-readiness per grade class. SAM masks support multi-trait phenotyping (diameter, curvature, defects). Robotic deployment and cross-crop evaluation (courgettes, aubergines, green beans) would establish generalisability. 

# 7 Conclusion 

This paper presented CucumberVision, an open-source framework evaluating five length measurement meth-ods (M1–M5) within a unified D435 + YOLO26n + SAM ViT-B pipeline. The proposed M5 medial arc spline fitting a cubic spline through 3D medial-axis vertebrae and computing arc length by trapezoidal in-tegration achieves the best accuracy: MAE 0.58 cm, RMSE 0.61 cm, MAPE 4.13% (95% CI: 2.15–3.89%), 

R2 = 0 .98 (r = 0 .998 ), significantly outperforming all methods ( p < 0.001 , Bonferroni-corrected). The five-method comparison reveals a monotonic accuracy–complexity trade-off from M1 (9.68%) to M5 (4.13%) the first such evidence under matched conditions. The intrinsic alignment correction eliminates 12–18% systematic underestimation. The adaptive selector guarantees 100% coverage. CucumberVision is deployed as a Streamlit dashboard with live and offline modes. Framework, dataset, and models will be released as open source.
